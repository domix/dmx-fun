Since chapter 1, a full course has been an error: `CourseFull`. Then the business rule changes.
A student who finds the course full is not turned away; they join a waiting list and are told
their position. Nothing went wrong. The student simply gets a different answer.

## Not every second answer is a failure

The temptation is to keep `CourseFull` and attach the position to it. That makes the waiting
list an error the caller has to "recover" from, and it puts something that is not a failure on
the failure track, where `recoverWith` and `mapError` would treat it as one. A model that
matches the business has two success shapes:

<Outcome/>

`Either<Waitlisted, Enrolled>` says exactly that. Unlike `Result`, `Either` is **neutral**:
neither side is the good one. `Left` and `Right` are just two legitimate outcomes, and which goes
on which side is a convention you pick and document. `Result` remains the type for the failure
track; the two compose, as the return type now shows: `Result<Either<Waitlisted, Enrolled>, EnrollmentError>`.

## The step that forks

<Seat/>

One step, two exits. A full course returns `Left` with the position and never reaches `charge`,
which is the real business consequence: nobody is billed for a spot in a queue. An available
seat goes through payment and persistence as before and returns `Right`.

## The chain

<Enroll/>

The pipeline is the same shape as chapter 5 with one fewer step; `ensureSeatAvailable` was
folded into the fork. `EnrollmentError` lost `CourseFull` and gained nothing: the error type got
**smaller** as the model came closer to the business rule, which is a good sign that the case
never belonged there. The `AlreadyEnrolled` recovery now returns the existing enrollment as a `Right`.

## The caller, with nested patterns

<Describe/>

Record patterns reach through both layers: `Result.Ok(Either.Right(Enrolled(var e)))`. The
`switch` is still exhaustive over both the outer `Result` and the inner `Either`. Callers who
do not care which side they got can use `fold(onLeft, onRight)`, which treats both sides alike;
there is no `getOrElse` here because there is nothing to fall back from.

## When to reach for Either

Use `Either` when both outcomes are valid and neither is "the problem": enrolled or waitlisted,
cash or card, cached or fetched. Use `Result` when one side is a failure you may want to recover
from, map, or log as such. If you catch yourself naming the left side `...Error`, you want
`Result`. The reasoning is in <a href={`${base}adr/adr-007-either-neutral`}>ADR-007</a>.

## What changed since chapter 5

- `enroll` returns `Result<Either<Waitlisted, Enrolled>, EnrollmentError>`.
- `CourseFull` is gone from `EnrollmentError`; a full course is a `Left`, and it is never charged.
- The caller's `switch` matches through `Result` and `Either` in one pattern.
