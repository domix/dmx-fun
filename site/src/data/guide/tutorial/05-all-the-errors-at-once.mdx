So far `enroll` received clean ids. Real enrollments start with a form, and forms arrive as
strings. A user who gets their email, age and id wrong wants to hear about all three, not fix
one, submit, and discover the next.

<Form/>

## Where the two tracks stop helping

A `Result` chain stops at the first failure; that is its whole point. For a form it is the
wrong behaviour: three bad fields would surface one at a time. `Validated` is the type for this
case. Each field check produces a `Validated`, and combining them keeps **every** error:

<ByHand/>

`combine` takes the other check, a way to merge errors, and a way to merge values. When both
sides are valid, the values merge; when either is invalid, the errors do. `NonEmptyList::concat`
is the merger, because a list of errors that exists is never empty. Three bad fields produce one
`Invalid` with three messages, in field order.

It works, and the pattern is already repeating: a predicate, a message, a ternary. By the fifth
form you will have written it twenty times.

## Rules as values

<Guards/>

A `Guard` is one rule: a predicate and the message for when it fails. `NOT_BLANK`, `EMAIL` and
`AGE_AT_LEAST_16` are written once and know nothing about forms. `contramap` points a rule at a
field and prefixes the message with the field name. `allOf` runs every rule and collects every
failure, which is exactly what `byHand` did with `combine`, minus the ceremony. The same guards
can validate the next form, a request body, or a CSV row.

`Guard.ofCatching` is chapter 4 at the scale of a single rule: `parseInt` throws on `"fifteen"`,
and the exception is folded into the rule's message instead of escaping.

## The bridge into the pipeline

<Bridge/>

`toResult` turns the `Validated` into a `Result`, and `mapError` wraps the message list into
`InvalidForm`, the sixth case of `EnrollmentError`. From there the chapter 4 pipeline runs
unchanged. A bad form never reaches the catalog or the gateway.

Notice the boundary this draws. Form errors are **messages for a user**, accumulated, and they
live in `NonEmptyList<String>`. Everything after the form is a **typed domain error** meant for
code to switch on. Keeping the two apart is what lets the pipeline stay exhaustive while the
form stays flexible.

## What changed since chapter 4

- `enroll` takes a raw `EnrollmentForm`; validation is the first step of the chain.
- Field checks accumulate with `Validated`, first by hand, then as reusable `Guard` values.
- `EnrollmentError.InvalidForm` carries all the messages into the failure track.
